  `#electrical`, `#roofing`.
- Post images from `/public`: `ad-square.png`, `ad-landscape.png`,
  `showcase.mp4` (35s demo, captions on, strongest asset).

## 7. Threads

### Thread A — "Why homeowners ghost your estimate"

1/ A homeowner who ghosts you usually wasn't shopping you. She got busy, your
email slid down the inbox, and by the time she looked again someone else had
already started.

2/ Speed is most of it. The quote that shows up while she's still standing in
the garage thinking about the problem wins jobs the Thursday quote never gets
a shot at.

3/ Trust is the other half. Your logo, your license number, itemized labor and
materials, a total she can read. Not a number scribbled on the back of an
invoice.

4/ Then the part almost nobody does: following up. Twice, politely, then
stop. Ask any contractor how many of their open quotes got a second message.
The answer is usually none.

5/ That's what Toolbelt automates. Type the job in plain English, send a
branded e-signable estimate in about a minute, and it does the nudging on its
own.
crewcraftapp.com

### Thread B — "What belongs on a panel upgrade estimate"

1/ Quoting a 100A → 200A panel swap. Here's what a homeowner should see on the
page, and what happens when they don't.

2/ The panel itself, the breakers, the grounding kit. Line items, priced
separately. When it's one lump sum, every competitor's lump sum looks
comparable, and the cheapest one wins.

3/ Permit coordination, named. It's real work and real time. Bury it in labor
and you're paying for it out of your own pocket.

4/ Your load assumptions, in writing. If the EV charger goes in next year, the
assumptions you made today are the ones that get argued about.

5/ Your license number on the document. Half of what a homeowner is buying is
the belief that you're the real thing.

6/ Toolbelt writes all five from one typed sentence, prices them off your own
price book, and sends it e-signable.
crewcraftapp.com/#electrical

### Thread C — "The paperwork tax"

1/ Nobody quits the trades because the work is hard. They quit because the
work ends at five and the paperwork ends at nine.

2/ Write the quote. Format the quote. Send the quote. Chase the quote. Turn it
into an invoice. Chase the invoice. Ask for the review. None of it is billable
and all of it is required.

3/ Every one of those steps is a place a job quietly dies. The quote you meant
to write tonight. The invoice you meant to send Friday. The review you never
asked for.

4/ So the belt does them. Estimate in 60 seconds, invoice created the second
they sign, follow-ups on a timer, review ask when the payment clears.

5/ You get the evening back. That's the product.
crewcraftapp.com
